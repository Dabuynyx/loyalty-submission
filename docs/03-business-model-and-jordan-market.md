# 03 — Business Model, Monetization & Jordan Market Strategy

## 1. Jordan Competitive Landscape

Ride-hailing in Jordan is regulated by the **Land Transport Regulatory Commission (LTRC)**, which has licensed multiple platforms and lifted past fleet caps, intensifying competition:
- **Careem & Uber:** Established leaders in Amman with broad coverage and Careem's super-app ecosystem (Rides, Food, Pay, DineOut).
- **Jeeny:** Aggressive pricing and frequent rider/driver promos.
- **Petra Ride & Gojo:** Homegrown Jordanian competitors competing on local branding and lower commissions.

**The Core Problem:** Drivers in Jordan face high fuel costs, vehicle maintenance, and LTRC compliance fees. With multiple apps on their phone, they switch to whichever app offers the best short-term bonus — costing Careem high **Driver Acquisition Cost (CAC)** and causing peak-hour shortages.

## 3. How Careem Makes Money (Unit Economics)

A common executive question is: *"If Careem's commission drops from 60% down to 40% at Tier 6, how does Careem make more money?"*

### Five Profit Drivers

1. **Volume & Utilization Expansion (The Core Math)**
   - A casual/churning Tier-1 driver completes ~100 rides/month (JOD 500 gross fares) at 60% Careem share = **JOD 300 gross margin**, then churns (requiring ~JOD 80–120 CAC to replace).
   - A retained Tier-6 Legend driver completes ~350 rides/month (JOD 1,750 gross fares) at 40% Careem share = **JOD 700 gross margin** — **2.3x more total JOD profit per driver** despite the lower percentage!

2. **Blended Portfolio Take-Rate**
   - Not every driver is at Tier 6. New, part-time, and returning drivers sit in T1–T3 (60%–52% Careem share), keeping the **blended platform take-rate around 48%–52%**.

3. **High-Margin Subscription Revenue**
   - **Captain Pro Pass (JOD 9.99/season):** Pure software margin; if 25% of active drivers subscribe, it adds substantial predictable monthly recurring revenue (MRR).
   - **Careem Plus (JOD 2.99/month):** Drives higher ride frequency (10%–15% cashback keeps riders locked into Careem) and cross-sells Careem Food.

4. **Partner-Sponsored Rewards (Zero Cost to Careem)**
   - Local Jordanian fuel stations, oil/tire shops, and cafés sponsor Tier 2–5 vouchers in exchange for guaranteed driver/rider foot traffic.

5. **Careem Pay Float & Cashless Savings**
   - Incentivizing Careem Pay with +25% XP reduces cash-collection defaults and builds digital wallet float.

## 4. Jordan Market Reality & Commission Calibration Note

> **Important Business & Calibration Disclosure**  
> In Jordan's real-world independent ride-hailing market, standard platform commissions are typically **20%–25%** (meaning drivers keep 75%–80% before fuel and car expenses).  
>
> **How the 60% → 40% Careem Split Fits:**
> - **Option A — Careem-Managed / Financed Fleet Model:** The **60% Careem / 40% Driver → 40% Careem / 60% Driver** curve is commercially realistic when Careem (or a fleet partner) provides the **vehicle, insurance, LTRC licensing, and maintenance**, and the driver provides labor.
> - **Option B — Independent Car-Owner Calibration:** For independent drivers using their own cars in Jordan, the exact same Battle Pass engine and tier curve can be calibrated via configuration to **28% Careem / 72% Driver (Tier 1) → 14% Careem / 86% Driver (Tier 6)** so Careem remains competitive against Jeeny and Petra Ride.

Because the tier table is **configuration-driven**, Careem can run **both** curves simultaneously (Fleet Track vs. Independent Captain Track) from a single platform.
